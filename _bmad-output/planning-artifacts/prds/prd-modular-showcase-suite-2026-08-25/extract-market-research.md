## 2. The Seams: Where Each Family Fails the Other's Customers

### What full-suite school platforms do badly for activity clubs
- **No skill/level progression model.** Clubs need belt/level systems, skill checklists, and competition tracking as first-class objects — school gradebooks (letter grades, GPA, transcripts) repurposed for this are a poor fit.
- **Wrong billing model.** Clubs need rolling/seasonal enrollment, tryout registration, early-bird pricing, waitlists, make-up lessons, family-level invoicing (one invoice per family across programs), automatic sibling discounts, and multi-sport bundles — school platforms bill per-term tuition per student.
- **Wrong scheduling model.** Clubs run short recurring class slots with instructor substitution, planned-absence handling, and make-up scheduling; school timetabling engines assume semester timetables. Only some club tools (Daxko is the one explicitly advertising it) handle **instructor substitution** — this validates coach substitution as an under-served feature even inside the club-software category.
- **Coach-side mobile workflow** (poolside attendance, skill sign-off on a phone) is a club-tool norm that school platforms lack.

### What club-management tools lack that schools need
- **No academic layer**: no real gradebook/transcripts, no LMS content/curriculum delivery, no assignments — a swim school that also runs academic or structured-curriculum programs outgrows them.
- **Weak reporting**: iClassPro's reporting is "very detailed but extremely difficult to pull what you want"; users bolt on extra tools for communications, marketing, waivers, and forms (Activity Messenger exists specifically to patch this gap).
- **Communication reliability problems**: reviewers report group messages not reaching signed-up parents and group-billing glitches.
- **Clunky mobile experiences** on both Jackrabbit and iClassPro (per comparison reviews) — despite mobile being the dominant parent channel; hence franchises building their own apps.
- **Pricing that climbs with enrollment** ($4.50–$6/student/mo) penalizes growth; feature-gating to higher tiers (iClassPro Pro) is a recurring complaint.
- **No multi-entity administration** for franchise/branch operations beyond basic location support; no white-labeling.

**Implication for OneEdu:** the wedge is a single platform where the *institution type* (school vs. club) selects a discipline module that swaps the progression model (grades ↔ skill levels), the scheduling model (timetable ↔ class slots + substitution), and the billing model (tuition ↔ family/seasonal), while sharing SIS, messaging, finance, and admin. No incumbent on either side offers that switch natively; OpenEduCat marketing a separate "sports academy" edition of a school ERP is the closest signal that vendors see the same seam.

## 3. Table Stakes for a Production School Platform in 2026

### 3a. Compliance & child-data protection (hard requirements)
- **COPPA (US, amended rule):** effective June 23, 2025 with **full compliance deadline April 22, 2026** — i.e., already in force at research date. Requires: **separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosures/advertising**, a **written data-retention policy** (retain children's data only as long as necessary, then delete), and an **expanded definition of personal information** (incl. biometric identifiers). School-authorization (FERPA-aligned) consent path is recognized for edtech.
- **FERPA (US):** baseline for education records; vendors operate as "school officials" under district agreements. FERPA-vs-COPPA interplay is now a standard vendor diligence question.
- **UK/EU:** UK **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** (effective June 19, 2025) embeds the **Age Appropriate Design Code's child-centric privacy-by-design into UK GDPR Art. 25(1) as a binding requirement**, and mandates new codes of practice for **EdTech and for AI/automated decision-making**. Edtech providers serving schools can be in scope of the Children's Code even though schools themselves are not. Expected practice: early DPIAs, age assurance, isolated children's-data pipelines, audit trails.
- **State-level US student privacy laws** (SOPIPA-style) add per-state contractual requirements — a multi-tenant platform needs per-tenant data-processing configurability.
- **Practical PRD consequences:** data-retention/deletion policy engine, per-tenant consent management, third-party-sharing consent gates, audit logs, DPIA-ready documentation, child-appropriate defaults (no behavioral ads, minimal collection).

### 3b. Parent-facing mobile + payments (expected, not differentiating)
- Dedicated **parent mobile app** showing grades, assignments, attendance, news; **automated alerts** (attendance, low grades) via push/text/voice.
- **Cashless payments as the norm**: ACH + cards + **Apple Pay / Google Pay** inside the parent portal, no separate login; tuition and non-tuition fees, invoices, donations. "As seamless as ordering groceries" is the stated 2026 parent expectation.
- **Security baseline**: role-based access (admin/teacher/parent/student), SSO, MFA, audit logs.
- Buyers explicitly moving away from fragmented point tools toward **all-in-one portals** (SIS + login + parent portal + admissions + billing).

## 4. Multi-Tenancy & White-Labeling as a Selling Point

- **Multi-tenant + white-label is an established, marketed category in LMS** (CYPHER Learning, Paradiso, Docebo, LMS Portals): one account manages many branded portals; each tenant sees only its own branding; cited benefits = shared-infrastructure cost reduction, centralized management, resell model ("resell fully branded portals … keep 100% revenue").
- **In school management specifically it is emerging and franchise-driven**: white-label school management is pitched at **franchise brands and multi-school groups** wanting brand-level digital experience without custom development — multi-branch management, curriculum tracking, parent comms under the group's own brand (logos, colors, fonts, **custom domains, email templates, mobile UI**).
- **Real-world validation from the swim segment**: Goldfish and Big Blue (large US swim franchises) built proprietary mobile apps because off-the-shelf club tools could not deliver a branded experience — a white-labeled native app per tenant directly addresses this.
- PRD takeaway: OneEdu's multi-tenant admin should treat **tenant-level branding (incl. the native mobile app experience), per-tenant module activation, per-tenant compliance configuration, and group/franchise roll-up reporting** as the selling points; "multi-tenancy" alone is infrastructure, the sellable feature is branded-per-tenant + centrally-governed.

## 5. Synthesis for the OneEdu PRD

1. **Positioning wedge is real**: school suites and club tools are separate product families with documented mutual blind spots; nobody credibly serves an operator running both academic and activity programs (or a group owning both kinds of institutions) on one platform.
2. **Modularity is table stakes; the discipline-module concept is the differentiator** — a module that swaps progression/scheduling/billing semantics per discipline goes beyond incumbents' "toggle features on/off."
3. **Coach substitution + training summary reports are defensible first features**: substitution is explicitly advertised by only one mainstream club platform (Daxko), and AI-personalized training/lesson reporting is only "early-stage among premium operators."
4. **Price anchors**: club tools ~$4.50–$6/active student/mo + base fee; school SIS $2–$15/student/mo or $100–$900/mo flat tiers; modular per-module pricing (Classter/OpenEduCat model) fits OneEdu's architecture.
5. **Compliance is a 2026 gate, not a nice-to-have**: amended COPPA (in full effect Apr 2026) data-retention/deletion + separate third-party consent, UK DUAA/Children's Code privacy-by-design, and forthcoming EdTech + AI codes of practice must be designed in (per-tenant).
6. **Parity checklist**: parent native app with push alerts + in-app payments (cards/ACH/wallets), SSO/MFA/RBAC/audit logs, online booking (dominant club enrollment channel), coach/teacher mobile workflows, NL analytics + early-warning AI.
7. **White-label-per-tenant (web + native app) targets the proven franchise pain** that drove big swim brands to build proprietary apps.
