# Digest: Higher Education / Australia, Singapore, Malaysia

Run notes: about 21 tool calls. Australian government sites (education.gov.au, tcsisupport.gov.au, legislation.gov.au full text) timed out or returned only the table of contents when fetched. Several AU claims therefore rest on provider policies that quote the National Code, or on search excerpts from official pages. Search-excerpt evidence is labelled as such below.

## Findings

### AUSTRALIA

- [AU][attendance] The National Code of Practice for Providers of Education and Training to Overseas Students 2018 (F2017L01182) Standard 8 ("Overseas student visa requirements") requires every registered provider to monitor **course progress**. It also requires providers to monitor **attendance where applicable** (Standard 8.1 as quoted by providers). | source: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text (only the table of contents loaded); https://policies.mq.edu.au/document/view.php?id=57 | Federal Register of Legislation; Macquarie University | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: attendance
- [AU][attendance] Attendance monitoring is required for **school, ELICOS and Foundation Program** courses. Clause 8.6 (as quoted by Navitas English): "the registered provider of a school, ELICOS or Foundation Program course must have and implement a documented policy and process for monitoring and recording attendance". **Higher education award courses are not attendance-monitored.** HE uses course-progress monitoring only. The University of Canberra procedure says attendance "is not required for Higher Education level courses – only ELICOS, Foundation Program and VET-related courses". VET attendance is monitored only where the ESOS agency (ASQA) requires it. | source: https://navitasenglish.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/policy-procedure-student-attendance-nes-9.01.pdf ; https://policies.canberra.edu.au/document/view-current.php?id=12 ; https://www.education.gov.au/download/11460/standard-8-overseas-student-visa-requirements/29923/document/docx (search excerpt) | Navitas; University of Canberra; Dept of Education | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: verified (two independent providers plus an excerpt from the Department factsheet) | class: attendance
- [AU][attendance] The threshold is **at least 80% of scheduled contact hours** per study period, or higher where state or territory law requires it (clause 8.6.1 per Navitas). Providers must intervene with students at risk before attendance falls below 80%. **Exception:** for school and ELICOS courses, a provider may decide not to report a student who is below 80% for compassionate or compelling reasons, provided attendance stays at or above **70%** (clause 8.15). | source: Dept of Education Standard 8 factsheet (search excerpt) https://www.education.gov.au/download/11460/standard-8-overseas-student-visa-requirements/29923/document/docx ; https://edithcowancollege.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/attendance-policy.pdf ; https://jcu.edu.au/policy/other/student-services/attendance-monitoring-policy-english-language-programs | Dept of Education; ECU College; JCU | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: verified | class: attendance
- [AU][attendance] The reporting workflow is a **Notice of Intention to Report** (the student falls below 80% or makes unsatisfactory progress), then **20 working days** for the student to appeal through internal complaints and appeals, then a report to the Department via PRISMS if the student does not appeal or the appeal fails. Macquarie's policy (clauses 24, 26, 34) has a separate notice for students below 70%. | source: https://policies.mq.edu.au/document/view.php?id=57 | Macquarie University | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: attendance
- [AU][data-return] Reporting under ESOS Act 2000 s19 (for example, a student not commencing, ceasing, or breaching visa conditions) is lodged through **PRISMS** (Provider Registration and International Student Management System). PRISMS also issues the **Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)**. I believe the s19 notice must be given within 14 days of the event (with a 31-day window for some notices), but I could not confirm this because the legislation text did not load. | source: none read | n.d. | confidence: unverified | class: data-return
- [AU][regulatory] **National Planning Level (NPL) for 2026: 295,000** new overseas student commencements, 25,000 more than the 2025 NPL of 270,000. From 2026, students moving to public universities from Australian secondary schools, affiliated pathway providers or TAFEs are exempt from the NPL. Visa-processing priority is set by a Ministerial Direction. MD 111 is being replaced; the number "MD 115 effective 14 Nov 2025" comes from one commercial site only. | source: https://www.ieaa.org.au/IEAA/News/2025/Australian-Government-2026-National-Planning-Level.aspx ; https://www.education.gov.au/node/19310 (search excerpt) ; https://www.thisisaustralia.com/news/2026-student-visa-caps/ | IEAA; Dept of Education; commercial site | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: verified (NPL 295,000) / unverified (MD 115) | class: regulatory
- [AU][regulatory] TEQSA regulates higher education against the Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021. ESOS agencies are TEQSA for HE and ASQA for VET and ELICOS. TEQSA's annual information collection points HE providers to TCSI for student data. | source: https://teqsa.gov.au/node/30 (search excerpt) | TEQSA | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: regulatory
- [AU][data-return] **TCSI (Tertiary Collection of Student Information)** is the HE (and VET Student Loans) data collection run under HESA 2003. Reporting is **event-based** and organised in "packets" (student, course admission, unit enrolment, and so on). Each packet's deadline is set in the data packets and in the "2026 Instrument for the Higher Education Data Collection". Reporting more than 6 months before a unit's census date is optional. **Corrections must be made before the initial deadline or within 7 days of the information becoming available, whichever is later.** Late reporting may breach HESA and affect payments. A separate HE Staff collection also runs through TCSI. | source: https://www.tcsisupport.gov.au/reporting/hestudent/requirements (search excerpt; direct fetch timed out) ; https://www.tcsisupport.gov.au/news-centre/tcsi-january-newsletter-2025 | Dept of Education (TCSI Support) | 2025–2026 | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: data-return
- [AU][identifier] **USI is required for HE students.** From 1 Jan 2023, students requesting any Commonwealth assistance (CSP, HECS-HELP, FEE-HELP, OS-HELP, SA-HELP) must have a USI. From 2023, **all** students, whatever their commencement date, need a USI to graduate and receive their award unless exempt. **Onshore international HE students need a USI to receive their award. Offshore international students currently do not.** Providers verify USIs through the USI Organisation Portal or USI web services in their student management system. Name and date of birth must match the USI record. | source: https://www.education.gov.au/higher-education-publications/higher-education-administrative-information-providers-october-2021/36-reporting-requirements (node/12866, search excerpt) | Dept of Education | Oct 2021 (HEAIP) | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: identifier

### SINGAPORE

- [SG][attendance] The ICA Student's Pass Terms & Conditions require the holder "to achieve at least **90% of the overall attendance**" at the approved institution. | source: https://www.ica.gov.sg/docs/default-source/ica/files/docs/terms_-_conditions_stp.pdf (search excerpt) | ICA | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely | class: attendance
- [SG][attendance] **School undertaking on ICA eForm V36A (Information on School):** the school "undertakes the responsibility to monitor the attendance and performance of the applicant constantly". It must ensure the student (a) does not miss classes for **7 or more consecutive days** without valid reason, and (b) does not have **less than 90% attendance in any month** without valid reason. Otherwise the school must notify ICA, and the pass may be cancelled or not renewed. **No statutory day-count for notification was found.** Institutions say "immediately", and Genetic Computer School notifies ICA at the 7-day absence point. | source: https://www.ica.gov.sg/docs/default-source/ica/forms/form-v36a_application-for-stp_info-on-school.pdf (search excerpt) ; https://www.kaplan.com.sg/student-policies/ ; https://genetic.edu.sg/policies_procedures/attendance/ | ICA; Kaplan; Genetic | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: verified (ICA form plus independent PEIs agree on 90% monthly and 7 days) | class: attendance
- [SG][regulatory] The 90% threshold comes from **ICA** (immigration), not the Private Education Regulations. The **Private Education Regulations 2009 (S 617/2009)** require registered PEIs to keep student records, including name, NRIC or FIN, nationality, and attendance records. The regulator is the Committee for Private Education (CPE) under SkillsFuture Singapore, through the Private Education Act 2009. Under EduTrust, a PEI must hold EduTrust certification to enrol Student's Pass holders (my belief, not verified). | source: https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/PEA2009-S617-2009 (search excerpt) ; https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/PEA2009 | AGC Singapore Statutes Online | 2009 (amended to 2021) | accessed 2026-11-02 | confidence: likely (records duty) / unverified (EduTrust gate) | class: regulatory

## Contradictions / surprises
- **SG monthly vs overall 90%:** the ICA Student's Pass T&C says "90% of the overall attendance", but the school undertaking (eForm V36A) says "<90% in any month". A product should support both monthly and cumulative 90% checks, plus a 7-consecutive-day absence alert.
- **AU HE has no attendance-monitoring duty under ESOS.** Attendance features for an AU HE pack serve ELICOS and Foundation pathways and institutional policy, not HE award courses. The statutory requirement for HE is course-progress monitoring and intervention.
- **MY PDPA commencement:** Rajah & Tann places the breach-notification regime (and data portability) at 1 Apr 2025. DFDL and Baker McKenzie say 1 Jun 2025 for DPO and breach notification. Check against the gazette (P.U.(B) notice of 24 Dec 2024).
- **AU Ministerial Direction number** (MD 111 replaced by "MD 115"?) comes from a commercial site only.
- **CHESSN** remains a TCSI system identifier even as the USI replaces it as the primary identifier. A SIS may still need to store the CHESSN as read-only data returned from TCSI.
